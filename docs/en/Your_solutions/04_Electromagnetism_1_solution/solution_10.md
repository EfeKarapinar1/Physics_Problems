## 1. Key Definitions and Formulas

### Lorentz Force (on a wire)
When an electric current flows through a wire placed within an external magnetic field, the moving charges (electrons) experience a force. The sum of these individual forces results in a macroscopic force on the wire itself.

### The Formula
The magnitude of the magnetic force $F$ exerted on a straight wire of length $L$ carrying a current $I$ in a uniform magnetic field $B$ is given by:

$$
F = I \cdot L \cdot B \cdot \sin(\theta)
$$

Where:
* **$F$**: Magnetic force measured in Newtons (N).
* **$I$**: Current flowing through the wire in Amperes (A).
* **$L$**: Length of the wire in meters (m).
* **$B$**: Magnetic field strength in Teslas (T).
* **$\theta$**: The angle between the direction of the current and the direction of the magnetic field.



### Physical Significance of the Angle
* **Maximum Force**: Occurs when the wire is perpendicular to the field ($\theta = 90^\circ$).
* **Zero Force**: Occurs when the wire is parallel to the field ($\theta = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$), as the charges are moving along the field lines.

## 2. Problem Solution

**Given Data:**
* Length ($L$) = 2.0 m
* Current ($I$) = 10 A
* Magnetic Field ($B$) = 0.5 T

### a) Angle $\theta = 90^\circ$
In this scenario, the wire is perpendicular to the magnetic field. This is where the magnetic "push" is at its strongest because $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$.

$$
F = 10 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.5 \cdot \sin(90^\circ)
$$

$$
F = 10 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.5 \cdot 1
$$

**$F = 10\text{ N}$**

---

### b) Angle $\theta = 45^\circ$
When the wire is tilted at an angle, only the component of the magnetic field perpendicular to the wire contributes to the force. Since $\sin(45^\circ) \approx 0.707$:

$$
F = 10 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.5 \cdot \sin(45^\circ)
$$

$$
F = 10 \cdot 0.707
$$

**$F \approx 7.07\text{ N}$**

---

### c) Angle $\theta = 0^\circ$
Here, the wire is aligned perfectly with the magnetic field lines. Since the charges are moving in the same direction as the field, they experience no sideways deflection.

$$
F = 10 \cdot 2.0 \cdot 0.5 \cdot \sin(0^\circ)
$$

$$
F = 10 \cdot 0
$$

**$F = 0\text{ N}$**
