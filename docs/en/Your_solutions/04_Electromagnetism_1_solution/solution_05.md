## 1. Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### Electric Force ($F_e$)
The force exerted on a charged particle by an electric field. For a proton with charge $e$ in an electric field $E$, the force is:

$$
F_e = eE
$$

### Gravitational Force ($F_g$)
Also known as weight, this is the force exerted on a mass $m$ by gravity.

$$
F_g = m_p g
$$

### Equilibrium (Levitation)
For an object to levitate (remain stationary in mid-air), the net force acting on it must be zero. This means the upward electric force must exactly equal the downward gravitational force:

$$
F_e = F_g
$$

---

## 2. Given Constants

* **Mass of proton ($m_p$):** $1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* **Charge of proton ($e$):** $1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* **Gravitational acceleration ($g$):** $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Set up the equilibrium equation
We start by equating the two forces acting on the proton:

$$
eE = m_p g
$$

### Step 2: Isolate the Electric Field ($E$)
To find the required electric field strength, we rearrange the formula to solve for $E$:

$$
E = \frac{m_p g}{e}
$$

### Step 3: Substitute the known values
Now, we plug in the numerical values provided in the problem statement:

$$
E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}) \times (9.8 \text{ m/s}^2)}{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}}
$$

### Step 4: Perform the calculation
First, let's calculate the numerator (the weight of the proton):

$$
F_g \approx 1.6366 \times 10^{-26} \text{ N}
$$

Next, divide by the charge of the proton:

$$
E = \frac{1.6366 \times 10^{-26}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}
$$

$$
E \approx 1.022875 \times 10^{-7} \text{ V/m}
$$

---

## 4. Final Result

The electric field strength required to levitate a proton at the Earth's surface is approximately:

$$
E \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N/C (or V/m)}
$$

**Note:** The direction of the electric field must be **upward** because a proton is positively charged, and the electric force on a positive charge acts in the same direction as the field.
