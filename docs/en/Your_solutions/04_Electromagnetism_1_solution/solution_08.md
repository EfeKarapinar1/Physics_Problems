## 1. Definitions and Formulas

Before solving the problem, let's review the fundamental concepts required.

### Lorentz Force
The **Lorentz Force** is the force exerted on a charged particle moving through a magnetic and/or electric field. When only a magnetic field is present, the force depends on the charge, the velocity of the particle, and the strength of the magnetic field.



### The Formula
The magnitude of the magnetic Lorentz force is given by the following equation:

$$
F = |q| \cdot v \cdot B \cdot \sin(\theta)
$$

Where:
* **$F$**: Magnitude of the force (measured in Newtons, **N**).
* **$q$**: Electric charge of the particle (measured in Coulombs, **C**).
* **$v$**: Velocity of the particle (measured in meters per second, **m/s**).
* **$B$**: Magnetic flux density (measured in Teslas, **T**).
* **$\theta$**: The angle between the velocity vector and the magnetic field vector.

---

## 2. Problem Data

From the problem description, we can extract the following known values:

* **Charge ($q$):** $2 \times 10^{-19}$ C
* **Mass ($m$):** $4 \times 10^{-27}$ kg (Note: While given, mass is not required to find the *force*, only the resulting acceleration).
* **Magnetic Field ($B$):** $0.5$ T
* **Velocity ($v$):** $10^6$ m/s
* **Angle ($\theta$):** $90^\circ$ (since the particle enters "perpendicular to the field").

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Determine the sine of the angle
Since the particle enters the field perpendicularly, the angle $\theta$ is $90$ degrees.

$$
\sin(90^\circ) = 1
$$

This means the force is at its maximum possible value for this specific charge, speed, and field.

### Step 2: Substitute values into the Lorentz formula
We plug our known values into the simplified magnitude formula:

$$
F = (2 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}) \cdot (10^6 \text{ m/s}) \cdot (0.5 \text{ T}) \cdot 1
$$

### Step 3: Perform the calculation
First, let's group the numerical coefficients and the powers of ten:

1.  Multiply the coefficients: $2 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0.5 = 1 \cdot 10^6$
2.  Combine with the charge exponent: $1 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 10^{-19} = 10^{-13}$

$$
F = 1 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}
$$

---

## 4. Final Answer

The magnitude of the Lorentz force acting on the particle is:

$$
F = 10^{-13} \text{ N}
$$

**Explanation:** Even though the mass was provided, it is only necessary if you were asked to find the **acceleration** ($a = F/m$) or the **radius** of the circular path. To find the force itself, we only need the charge, velocity, and field strength.
