# Problem 7: Cyclotron Motion Solution

## Problem Statement
An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of **5000 V**. It enters a region of uniform magnetic field **B = 0.1 T**, perpendicular to its velocity. Calculate the radius of the circular path.

## 1. Relevant Formulas

The velocity $v$ acquired by an electron accelerated through potential $V$:

$$
eV = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}}
$$

The radius $r$ of the path in a magnetic field $B$:

$$
r = \frac{mv}{eB}
$$

## 2. Given Values
* Potential Difference ($V$): $5000 \text{ V}$
* Magnetic Field ($B$): $0.1 \text{ T}$
* Mass of electron ($m$): $9.11 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$
* Charge of electron ($e$): $1.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$

## 3. Calculation Steps

### Step A: Calculate Velocity
Substituting the values into the energy equation:

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 5000}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}}
$$

$$
v \approx 4.19 \times 10^7 \text{ m/s}
$$

### Step B: Calculate Radius
Using the cyclotron radius formula:

$$
r = \frac{(9.11 \times 10^{-31}) \times (4.19 \times 10^7)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 0.1}
$$

$$
r \approx 0.002386 \text{ m}
$$

## Final Answer
The radius of the circular path is **2.39 mm**.
