## 1. Definitions and Fundamental Formulas

To solve this problem, we must use the fundamental laws governing electrostatic and gravitational interactions.

### Coulomb’s Law
Describes the electrostatic force ($F_e$) between two point charges.

$$
F_e = k_e \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}
$$

* **$k_e$**: Coulomb's constant $\approx 8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$
* **$q$**: Magnitude of the charge.
* **$r$**: Distance between particles.

### Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation
Describes the gravitational force ($F_g$) between two masses.

$$
F_g = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}
$$

* **$G$**: Gravitational constant $\approx 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$
* **$m$**: Mass of the particles.

### Physical Constants
The following constants are used for the calculation:

* **Elementary charge ($e$):** $1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
* **Mass of a proton ($m_p$):** $1.672 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$
* **Mass of an electron ($m_e$):** $9.109 \times 10^{-31} \text{ kg}$
* **Average distance ($r$):** $5.3 \times 10^{-11} \text{ m}$

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Calculate the Electric Force ($F_e$)
We substitute the values into Coulomb's Law. Both particles have a charge magnitude of $e$.

$$
F_e = (8.99 \times 10^9) \frac{(1.602 \times 10^{-19})^2}{(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2}
$$

1. Square the charge: $(1.602 \times 10^{-19})^2 \approx 2.566 \times 10^{-38}$
2. Square the distance: $(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2 \approx 2.809 \times 10^{-21}$
3. Result:

$$
F_e \approx 8.21 \times 10^{-8} \text{ N}
$$

### Step 2: Calculate the Gravitational Force ($F_g$)
Now, we calculate the gravitational pull using the masses of the proton and electron.

$$
F_g = (6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \frac{(1.672 \times 10^{-27})(9.109 \times 10^{-31})}{(5.3 \times 10^{-11})^2}
$$

1. Product of masses: $\approx 1.523 \times 10^{-57}$
2. Result:

$$
F_g \approx 3.61 \times 10^{-47} \text{ N}
$$

### Step 3: Calculate the Ratio ($F_e / F_g$)
To find how many times stronger the electric force is than the gravitational force:

$$
\text{Ratio} = \frac{8.21 \times 10^{-8}}{3.61 \times 10^{-47}}
$$

$$
\text{Ratio} \approx 2.27 \times 10^{39}
$$

> **Conclusion:** The gravitational force between subatomic particles is so incredibly weak compared to the electromagnetic force that it is virtually always neglected in atomic and nuclear physics calculations.
