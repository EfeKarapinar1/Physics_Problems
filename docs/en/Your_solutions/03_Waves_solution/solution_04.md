# Phase Difference Calculation

## Problem

What is the phase difference in radians between two points on a wave that are separated by a distance of $\lambda/3$? 

---

## 1. Definitions and Formulas

### Wavelength ($\lambda$)
The **wavelength** is the spatial period of a periodic wave—the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is typically measured between two consecutive corresponding points, such as two peaks or two troughs.

### Phase Difference ($\Delta \phi$)
**Phase difference** describes the angular displacement between two points on a wave (or between two different waves). It tells us how much one point is "ahead" or "behind" another in its cycle. It is usually measured in **radians** ($0$ to $2\pi$) or **degrees** ($0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$).

### Path Difference ($\Delta x$)
The **path difference** is the actual physical distance between two points along the direction of the wave's propagation.

### The Relationship Formula
A full wavelength ($\lambda$) corresponds to a full cycle of $2\pi$ radians. Therefore, the ratio of the distance between two points to the total wavelength is proportional to the ratio of their phase difference to a full circle:

$$
\frac{\Delta \phi}{2\pi} = \frac{\Delta x}{\lambda}
$$

Rearranging to solve for the phase difference ($\Delta \phi$):

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot \Delta x
$$

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Solution



### Step 1: Identify the given values
From the problem description, we are given the separation distance (path difference) between the two points:

$$
\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{3}
$$

### Step 2: Plug the value into the formula
We substitute our value for $\Delta x$ into the general phase difference equation:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot \left( \frac{\lambda}{3} \right)
$$

### Step 3: Simplify the expression
Notice that the wavelength ($\lambda$) appears in both the numerator and the denominator, allowing them to cancel each other out:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi \cdot \lambda}{3 \cdot \lambda}
$$

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{3}
$$

### Step 4: Final Result
The phase difference is $\frac{2\pi}{3}$ radians.

---

## Final Answer
The phase difference between two points separated by a distance of $\lambda/3$ is:

$$
\Delta \phi = \frac{2\pi}{3} \text{ radians}
$$

*(Which is equivalent to $120^\circ$)*
