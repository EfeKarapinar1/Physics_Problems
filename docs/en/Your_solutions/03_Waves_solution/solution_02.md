# Physics Problem: String Harmonics

## Problem

A guitar string is 64 cm long and has a fundamental frequency (one antinode) of 330 Hz. What is the speed of the wave on this string?

## 1. Key Definitions and Formulas

Before solving the problem, we must understand the physical principles governing standing waves on a string.

### Definitions
* **Fundamental Frequency ($f_1$):** The lowest frequency of a vibrating object. In a string fixed at both ends, this occurs when the string forms a single "loop" or antinode.
* **Antinode:** The point of maximum displacement in a standing wave.
* **Harmonics:** Integer multiples of the fundamental frequency.
* **Wavelength ($\lambda$):** The spatial period of the wave—the distance over which the wave's shape repeats.

### Formulas
The relationship between the length of the string ($L$) and the wavelength ($\lambda$) for the $n$-th harmonic is:

$$
\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

For the **fundamental frequency** ($n = 1$), the formula simplifies to:

$$
\lambda_1 = 2L
$$

The wave speed ($v$) is related to frequency ($f$) and wavelength ($\lambda$) by the wave equation:

$$
v = f \cdot \lambda
$$

---

## 2. Problem Solving Steps

### Given Data:
* **Length ($L$):** $64\text{ cm} = 0.64\text{ m}$ (Converted to SI units)
* **Fundamental Frequency ($f_1$):** $330\text{ Hz}$
* **Harmonic Number ($n$):** $1$ (Since it is the fundamental frequency with one antinode)



### Step 1: Calculate the Wavelength
For the fundamental frequency, the string length represents exactly half of a wavelength ($L = \frac{1}{2}\lambda$). Therefore, we multiply the length by 2 to find the full wavelength.

$$
\lambda = 2 \cdot L
$$

Substituting the given value:

$$
\lambda = 2 \cdot 0.64\text{ m} = 1.28\text{ m}
$$

### Step 2: Calculate the Wave Speed
Now, we use the wave equation to find the speed ($v$) by multiplying the frequency by the wavelength we just calculated.

$$
v = f_1 \cdot \lambda
$$

Substituting the values:

$$
v = 330\text{ Hz} \cdot 1.28\text{ m}
$$

### Step 3: Final Calculation
Performing the multiplication:

$$
v = 422.4\text{ m/s}
$$

---

## 3. Final Answer

The speed of the wave on the guitar string is **$422.4\text{ m/s}$**.

> **Note:** This speed is typical for guitar strings, which usually have wave speeds ranging between $200\text{ m/s}$ and $500\text{ m/s}$ depending on their tension and linear mass density.
