# Standing Wave Modes Solution

## Problem 

A standing wave with four antinodes is produced on a string of length L = 80 cm. What is the wavelength of this wave?


## 1. Key Definitions and Formulas

Before solving the problem, it is essential to understand the physical principles of standing waves on a string fixed at both ends.

* **Standing Wave:** A wave pattern that results from the interference of two waves of the same frequency and amplitude traveling in opposite directions.
* **Antinode:** A point of maximum displacement (amplitude) in a standing wave.
* **Node:** A point of zero displacement in a standing wave.
* **Harmonic Number ($n$):** For a string fixed at both ends, the number of antinodes corresponds to the $n$-th harmonic mode.



### The Fundamental Formula
For a string of length $L$ fixed at both ends, the relationship between the length and the wavelength ($\lambda$) is given by:

$$
L = n \frac{\lambda_n}{2}
$$

Where:
* $L$ is the length of the string.
* $n$ is the number of antinodes (the harmonic number).
* $\lambda_n$ is the wavelength of the $n$-th harmonic.

Rearranging the formula to solve for **wavelength**:

$$
\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

---

## 2. Problem Solving Step-by-Step

**Problem:** A standing wave with four antinodes is produced on a string of length $L = 80 \text{ cm}$. What is the wavelength of this wave?

### Step 1: Identify the given values
* Length of the string ($L$): $80 \text{ cm}$
* Number of antinodes ($n$): $4$

### Step 2: Select the appropriate formula
We use the wavelength formula for the $n$-th harmonic:

$$
\lambda = \frac{2L}{n}
$$

### Step 3: Substitute the values into the formula
Now, we plug in our known values to calculate the wavelength:

$$
\lambda = \frac{2 \times 80 \text{ cm}}{4}
$$

### Step 4: Perform the final calculation
First, multiply the numerator:

$$
\lambda = \frac{160 \text{ cm}}{4}
$$

Then, divide by the number of antinodes:

$$
\lambda = 40 \text{ cm}
$$

---

## 3. Final Result

The wavelength of the standing wave is **$40 \text{ cm}$** (or $0.4 \text{ m}$).

> **Physical Interpretation:** In the 4th harmonic mode, exactly two full wavelengths fit within the length of the string ($2 \times 40 \text{ cm} = 80 \text{ cm}$).
