# Question - 5. Inelastic Collision

A 70 kg runner moving at $3 \text{ m/s}$ jumps onto a 140 kg stationary cart. What is the final speed of the cart with the runner? Is kinetic energy conserved in this collision? Explain.

---

## 1. Definitions and Formulas

Before solving the problem, let's review the fundamental physical concepts involved.

### Momentum
**Momentum ($p$)** is a vector quantity defined as the product of an object's mass and its velocity. It represents the "quantity of motion" an object has.

$$
p = m \cdot v
$$

### Law of Conservation of Momentum
In an isolated system (where no external forces act), the total momentum before a collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision.

$$
\sum p_{\text{initial}} = \sum p_{\text{final}}
$$

### Inelastic Collision
An **inelastic collision** is one in which the objects stick together after the impact or move with a common final velocity. While total momentum is conserved, the **total kinetic energy is not conserved** because some of it is transformed into other forms of energy (such as heat or sound).

### Kinetic Energy
**Kinetic energy ($KE$)** is the energy an object possesses due to its motion.

$$
KE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
$$

---

## 2. Problem Solving

### Problem Statement
> A 70 kg runner moving at 3 m/s jumps onto a 140 kg stationary cart. What is the final speed of the cart with the runner? Is kinetic energy conserved in this collision? Explain.

### Step 1: Identify Known and Unknown Variables

* **Mass of the runner ($m_1$):** $70 \text{ kg}$
* **Initial velocity of the runner ($v_1$):** $3 \text{ m/s}$
* **Mass of the cart ($m_2$):** $140 \text{ kg}$
* **Initial velocity of the cart ($v_2$):** $0 \text{ m/s}$ (stationary)
* **Final velocity of both ($v_f$):** $?$

### Step 2: Calculate the Final Speed
Using the **Law of Conservation of Momentum**, we know the momentum before must equal the momentum after:

$$
m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = (m_1 + m_2) v_f
$$

Substitute the known values into the equation:

$$
(70 \text{ kg} \cdot 3 \text{ m/s}) + (140 \text{ kg} \cdot 0 \text{ m/s}) = (70 \text{ kg} + 140 \text{ kg}) \cdot v_f
$$

$$
210 \text{ kg} \cdot \text{m/s} = 210 \text{ kg} \cdot v_f
$$

Solve for $v_f$:

$$
v_f = \frac{210}{210} = 1 \text{ m/s}
$$

**The final speed of the cart with the runner is $1 \text{ m/s}$.**

---

### Step 3: Analyze Kinetic Energy Conservation
To determine if kinetic energy is conserved, we compare the total kinetic energy before and after the collision.

#### Initial Kinetic Energy ($KE_i$):

$$
KE_i = \frac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2} m_2 v_2^2
$$

$$
KE_i = \frac{1}{2} (70) (3)^2 + 0
$$

$$
KE_i = 35 \cdot 9 = 315 \text{ J}
$$

#### Final Kinetic Energy ($KE_f$):

$$
KE_f = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 + m_2) v_f^2
$$

$$
KE_f = \frac{1}{2} (210) (1)^2
$$

$$
KE_f = 105 \text{ J}
$$

---

## 3. Conclusion and Explanation

**Is kinetic energy conserved?** No, kinetic energy is **not** conserved.

**Explanation:** The initial kinetic energy was $315 \text{ J}$, but the final kinetic energy is only $105 \text{ J}$. This means $210 \text{ J}$ of energy was "lost" during the collision. In an inelastic collision, this energy is typically converted into **heat (thermal energy)**, **sound**, and the **work** required to deform the objects or overcome friction as they lock together.
