# 8. Circular Motion: Centripetal Acceleration at the Equator

This document provides a step-by-step calculation of the centripetal acceleration experienced by a person at the Earth's equator due to the planet's rotation.

---

## 1. Necessary Definitions and Formulas

### Centripetal Acceleration ($a_c$)
The acceleration of an object moving in uniform circular motion. It is always directed toward the center of the circular path.

### Angular Velocity ($\omega$)
The rate at which an object rotates around a central axis, measured in radians per second ($\text{rad/s}$).

### Formulas

**1. Angular Velocity:**

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

* **$T$**: The period of one full rotation (in seconds).

**2. Centripetal Acceleration:**

$$
a_c = \omega^2 \cdot R
$$

* **$R$**: The radius of the circular path (in meters).

---

## 2. Given Data

* **Earth's Radius ($R$):** $6378 \text{ km}$
* **Rotation Period ($T$):** $1 \text{ day}$ (The time it takes for the Earth to complete one rotation).

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Unit Conversion (SI Units)
To ensure the final result is in $\text{m/s}^2$, we must convert the radius to meters and the period to seconds.

**Radius ($R$):**

$$
R = 6378 \text{ km} \times 1000 = 6,378,000 \text{ m}
$$

**Period ($T$):**

$$
T = 24 \text{ hours} \times 60 \text{ minutes} \times 60 \text{ seconds} = 86,400 \text{ s}
$$

### Step 2: Calculate Angular Velocity ($\omega$)
Using the converted period, we find how fast the Earth is spinning in radians:

$$
\omega = \frac{2\pi}{86,400} \approx 7.2722 \times 10^{-5} \text{ rad/s}
$$

### Step 3: Calculate Centripetal Acceleration ($a_c$)
Now, we plug our values for $\omega$ and $R$ into the centripetal acceleration formula:

$$
a_c = (7.2722 \times 10^{-5})^2 \times 6,378,000
$$

**First, square the angular velocity:**

$$
\omega^2 \approx 5.2885 \times 10^{-9} \text{ rad}^2/\text{s}^2
$$

**Now, multiply by the radius:**

$$
a_c \approx 5.2885 \times 10^{-9} \times 6,378,000
$$

$$
a_c \approx 0.0337 \text{ m/s}^2
$$

---

## 4. Final Conclusion

The centripetal acceleration of a person standing on the Earth's equator is approximately:

$$
a_c \approx 0.0337 \text{ m/s}^2
$$

> **Fun Fact:** This acceleration is very small compared to the acceleration due to gravity ($g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$). In fact, it is only about **0.34%** of gravity, which is why we don't feel like we are being flung into space!
